This is a project that I worked on before starting this blog. It actually was the project that made me wish I had documented more of the process. Unfortunately that also means that this page is criminally lacking in information, as I was not blogging as I went.

Take my words with a grain of salt, as I wrote most of this text about a year after actually finishing the guitar, and the guitar itself took about a year to make. 

The start of the inspiration for this project was this Belgian dude posting videos of his guitar making, [Tchik guitars](https://www.youtube.com/@tchiksguitars).

I'm not sure why I decided to build a guitar. I bought a router for a different project, and wanted to use it I guess. I can't even play guitar. But man was it fun to build and learn about. I think that, for me at least, building something is more about the journey, not the destination.

My boy tchiksguitars has a lovely 3 part tutorial for the beginner luthier.

Anyway, here is a bunch of pictures I had in my camera roll from this build, and my best memory of what the context of each one is.

## the Build

<img class="blogImage medium" src="/images/telecaster/laminated_body_template.jpg" alt="" onclick="toggleFullScreen(this)">

I used wood glue and a bunch of scrap wood I found in my dad's garage to laminate a big block to be used for the body. Filled in random holes and knots with CA glue or epoxy. 

<img class="blogImage medium" src="/images/telecaster/template.jpg" alt="" onclick="toggleFullScreen(this)">

I made some templates with a laser printer, some scrap plywood, and a jigsaw. (and also some sanding)

<img class="blogImage medium" src="/images/telecaster/neck_template.JPG" alt="" onclick="toggleFullScreen(this)">

I got a mahogany board from menards for the neck, and cut a slot down the middle (with a router) for the truss rod.

<img class="blogImage medium" src="/images/telecaster/routertime.JPG" alt="" onclick="toggleFullScreen(this)">

I then used some carpet tape to attack my templates to the wood, and cut them into the rough shapes of a neck and body. 

<img class="blogImage medium" src="/images/telecaster/routing_body.JPG" alt="" onclick="toggleFullScreen(this)">

I used a drill press with some forstner bits, along with the router, to hog out the body cavities. I also rounded the edges a bit.

<img class="blogImage medium" src="/images/telecaster/neck_head_lap.JPG" alt="" onclick="toggleFullScreen(this)">

Dry fitting and a long straightedge was used to fine tune the neck pocket.  

<img class="blogImage medium" src="/images/telecaster/neck_head_lap.JPG" alt="" onclick="toggleFullScreen(this)">

<img class="blogImage medium" src="/images/telecaster/wideshotrouter.JPG" alt="" onclick="toggleFullScreen(this)">

After that I lapped the headstock down to its proper depth. (with a router)

<img class="blogImage medium" src="/images/telecaster/chiselclean.JPG" alt="" onclick="toggleFullScreen(this)">

Then I clean up the router slop with my harbor freight chisels. 

<img class="blogImage medium" src="/images/telecaster/holding_neck.JPG" alt="" onclick="toggleFullScreen(this)">

I bought a pre-slotted fretboard from [mgbguitars.com](https://mgbguitars.com), and added the black inserts with a drill press. Then I put the truss-rod in the neck slot, added a dab of silicon, and glued the fretboard on.

<img class="blogImage medium" src="/images/telecaster/shinto_rasp.JPG" alt="" onclick="toggleFullScreen(this)">

Next I used a Japanese shinto rasp to cut the neck profiles at the top and the bottom of the neck. 

<img class="blogImage medium" src="/images/telecaster/shinto_prep.JPG" alt="" onclick="toggleFullScreen(this)">

This was a very interesting process to figure out geometrically, and the process I used to calculate the measurements of the curve is explained in tchiksguitars video. (We will come back and connect these 2 profiles later.) 

<img class="blogImage medium" src="/images/telecaster/fretboard.JPG" alt="" onclick="toggleFullScreen(this)">

Telecasters have a fretboard radius of 9.5 inches. You can sort of see it in the above image.

<img class="blogImage medium" src="/images/telecaster/sanding fret board.JPG" alt="" onclick="toggleFullScreen(this)">

To get this radius, I 3d printed a radius sanding block with 9.5 inches and got to sanding. 

I got radius and diameter confused when I sketched the sanding block in Fusion 360, and accidently sanded it to a 4.25 radius. Luckily I was able to print another block (with the proper size) and fix it!

<img class="blogImage medium" src="/images/telecaster/tuner_holes.JPG" alt="" onclick="toggleFullScreen(this)">

Then I drilled the holes for the tuner on the drill press.

<img class="blogImage medium" src="/images/telecaster/spokeshave1.JPG" alt="" onclick="toggleFullScreen(this)">

Now we can come back to the neck profiles. I used a spokeshave to connect the front and the back countours.

<img class="blogImage medium" src="/images/telecaster/spokeshave_chips.JPG" alt="" onclick="toggleFullScreen(this)">

I used a small straightedge along the length of neck to ensure i was staying straight, but as the top and bottom neck profiles are slightly different, I had to blend the curves by hand.

<img class="blogImage medium" src="/images/telecaster/spokeshave_sanding.JPG" alt="" onclick="toggleFullScreen(this)">

Sanding helped hide my sins with the spokeshave.

<img class="blogImage medium" src="/images/telecaster/spokeshave2.JPG" alt="" onclick="toggleFullScreen(this)">

Finished product after spokeshave and sanding.

<img class="blogImage medium" src="/images/telecaster/neck_carving_tape.JPG" alt="" onclick="toggleFullScreen(this)">

I used chisels and sandpaper (wrapped around a dowel) to shape the contours on the base of the neck.

<img class="blogImage medium" src="/images/telecaster/head_neck_chisel.JPG" alt="" onclick="toggleFullScreen(this)">

And did the same for the head of the neck.

<img class="blogImage medium" src="/images/telecaster/neck_carving_clamp.JPG" alt="" onclick="toggleFullScreen(this)">

Did this mostly by feel, which is funny as I dont know what a guitar neck should "feel" like.

<img class="blogImage medium" src="/images/telecaster/fender.jpg" alt="" onclick="toggleFullScreen(this)">

I wanted a custom logo with my name, that still looked like a fender guitar.

<img class="blogImage medium" src="/images/telecaster/kluesner_sticker.JPG" alt="" onclick="toggleFullScreen(this)">

I used a free vector art program, Inkscape, to sketch up a few designs.

<img class="blogImage medium" src="/images/telecaster/headstock sticker.JPG" alt="" onclick="toggleFullScreen(this)">

Then I printed my designs out on waterslide decal paper, and applied it to the headstock. (I put this sticker down ontop of tung oil, and polyurethane-ed over it. But I wish I would have put this sticker ontop of the first coat of poly instead as it would have blended better.

<img class="blogImage medium" src="/images/telecaster/finished_guitar_shop.JPG" alt="" onclick="toggleFullScreen(this)">

I used tung oil to finish the wood, and then wipe on polyurethane as a protective layer. Most of the hardware came from [bitterrootguitars.com](https://bitterrootguitars.com) at a decent price.
